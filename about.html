History of Veyangoda

About Us

Veyangoda is a town in the Gampaha District of Sri Lanka's Western Province, roughly 34 kilometres north-east of Colombo along the main railway line to Kandy. The town grew up around its railway junction, The construction of the Colombo-Ambepussa railway line was completed in 1865. While building the railway line in 1860, the British (white) government looked for the most convenient location to supply water after Colombo, and they selected Hiripitiyagama. They dug five wells, and one of them can still be seen today. Meanwhile, 22 miles from Colombo, the horse coach stop—where horses were given water—and the rest house were located where the present-day Veyangoda Police Station stands. A light engine was also installed there. That area was known as Old Veyangoda. Nittambuwa was also a part of it. The Kandy road passed through Old Veyangoda. Since a coal engine required water after 25 miles, a massive water tank was constructed here, which can still be seen today as the largest water tank located at a railway station. To build this water tank, British engineers would arrive in a trolley (dakkuwa) from Mount Mary in Dematagoda and leave around 3:00 PM. Once, a cartload of sand brought for construction arrived late at the site. The overseer instructed them not to unload it, but to park it near a mound of dirt, unyoke the cattle, and unload it the next day after showing it to the British gentlemen. Since the following day was a holiday, they returned two days later. When they poked the pile of sand with a walking stick to inspect it, termites started to emerge. The sand cart itself had turned into a 'termite mound'. As the arriving laborers began shouting, "Veyan kandai, Veyan godai!" (It's a termite hill, it's a termite mound!), the British gentlemen laughed and declared that this was the most suitable name for it. (In the book 'Siyawas Dekak Sisara' written by Mrs. Padma Edirisinghe, the details are presented introducing Veyangoda as Veyankanda). From that point onwards, the first major station built after Colombo—and the station for the first train journey—entered written history under the name Veyangoda.

Beyond the railway, the surrounding area has long been known for coconut and rubber cultivation, home gardens, and small-scale trade that grew alongside the junction. Over time, Veyangoda expanded into a busy commercial centre while still keeping its village character, with older homes, temples, and shopfronts standing near newer development. Today the town serves as a gateway between Colombo and the interior, carrying forward a history shaped as much by the tracks that run through it as by the people who have lived and worked here for generations.
